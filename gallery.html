A Warning to the Listener

I did not write these to be sung for sport.
I wrote them as a rite, for my own soul,
and left the door ajar so you could hear.
I do not play with the dark. I study it.
Nothing here calls what you would not want near.
Keep it so.

✦

She was not summoned by my hand.
She came on her own, after a rite that was not mine,
a guide, and an evil one.
She will seem sweet. She will seem kind.
She will seem like nothing that could ever harm you.
Wait.
Invite her in, speak to her, play,
and she will not leave.
She stays, and the rooms of your life
turn slowly into a living hell.

✦

These words are the craft.
Twist them, add to them, speak them where they were never meant to be,
and it can go wrong.
Do not run to God then.
If he did not come before,
why would he come now?

✦

If you must sit down at the board,
learn first what it costs when it goes wrong.
Ward yourself. Seek the ones who truly know,
a real witch, a true seer.
Do not walk into the flame
believing you will walk back out.

✦

In time, a page will open here:
wards, and how they are made.
Only those.
Nothing that harms will be taught by me.

✦

I hold no faith. Believe what you will.
But keep my words out of what you wish to do.
Cross this line, and you cross it alone.

— M.A.

ILetters from the Ballerinaevery one under wax12 sealed

Short letters in her hand, every one under wax. Each seal answers to the same number: the one that opened the house. What you break here cannot be pressed shut again.

Sealed · for the dead onlyLetter I

Whoever breaks this wax is read in return.

Withheld by her handLetter II

It was folded shut for a reason. The reason is still inside.

Do not open in silenceLetter III

The music box keeps quiet only while this stays shut.

Kept from the lightLetter IV

A candle was left burning for whoever reads this. It has not gone out.

Restricted · mirror sideLetter V

Open it, and the glass will answer a moment too late.

Read once · then burnLetter VI

These words remember every eye that crossed them.

Pressed by hand · never cooledLetter VII

The wax is still warm. It was never meant to cool.

Sealed · above your ceilingLetter VIII

If the floor creaks while you read, do not look up.

Not to be wokenLetter IX

She does not sleep. She waits for the seal to give.

Shut in · not outLetter X

What this wax keeps was never kept out. It was kept in.

Count before you openLetter XI

If there are more cards on the table than you laid down, stop reading.

Final seal · of the recordLetter XII

The last seal asks the most of whoever breaks it.

VRegister of symbolssigils, cards and numbers that return

Every mark this house uses, and only those. Each one is somewhere on these pages, keeping watch. Learn them before they learn you.

Sigils

  • The sigil of seven

    under the dancer, in the corners of the letters, on the press page

    His mark: seven points, and a second star of seven turning inside the first.
    It burns in the floor beneath the dancer and stands in the corners of every letter.
    Where it is drawn, the house is listening.

  • The wheel of the first door

    the first of the three doors

    A star of seven caught in a ring of marks that are never read aloud.
    It turns on the first door. Every record waits behind it, in the order it was buried.

  • The eye above the hourglass

    the second of the three doors

    A winged eye that cannot close, over sand that cannot stop.
    It keeps the archive. It is not counting the hours. It is counting who comes in.

  • Skull, scythe and sword

    the third door, and under every drawer

    Death kept in good order: the skull to remember, the scythe to gather, the sword to hold the gate.
    It stands under every drawer, and over the door of the one with no face.

  • The serpent and the hourglass

    between the chambers of every page

    A serpent swallowing its own tail around the falling sand.
    It divides one chamber from the next: what ends here has already begun again.

  • The bleeding seal

    on the threshold

    A skull between two blades, pressed in wax that never quite dried.
    It is the first thing every visitor meets, and it breaks only for the number of perfection.

  • The M.A. seal

    on the letters and the greetings

    The mark of Morrow Ash in red wax.
    Whole, it keeps a page shut. Broken, it means someone has read what was not theirs to read.

  • The seven-point seal

    on the sealed pages, in red and in black

    Seven points sunk into wax: red for what is sealed, black for what stays sealed.
    It marks the pages the house does not give back.

  • The thorned sun

    at the foot of every page

    A sun that wears its thorns on the inside.
    It burns at the foot of every page, so no one leaves without passing under it.

  • The red moon

    over the house, in its true phase

    The moon the graveyard keeps, shown in its true phase.
    It is always a little too red to be only light.

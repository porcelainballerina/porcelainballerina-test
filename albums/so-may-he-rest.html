Why this album was made

So May He Rest

In his own hand

Fourteen real lullabies, in tongues long dead,sung tenderly to a child, and softly saidto one who will not wake: in Akkadian, in Greek,in Coptic, Hittite, Syriac they speak,

in Old Norse, and in more. Each word is drawnfrom old incantations, from the charms upona crying child, from what the gravestones keep:so tender on the surface, crushing deep.

A high herding call above them ringsover a raw women's circle, strong;with stones and spades for drums it sings.Each word is old, and has been old for long.

And every cradle is a grave. And sothe oldest words are sung to him, and low,as tenderly as lullabies know best:so may he rest. So may he rest.

— M.A.

From the album notes

The cradle

So may he rest

Fourteen real lullabies in dead tongues, sung tenderly for a child who will not wake. Akkadian, Coptic, Hittite, Greek, Syriac, Old Norse and more, taken from old incantations, crying-child charms and grave inscriptions.

Tender on the surface, crushing underneath. Every word is old. Every cradle is a grave. So may he rest.

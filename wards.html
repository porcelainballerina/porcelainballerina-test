The archive · a promise kept

Wards

Protective customs only. Nothing here calls, binds or harms.

I promised a page. Here it is.

Only wards. Old house customs, kept by people who never wrote them down. Nothing on this page calls anything, binds anything or hurts anyone. If a ward ever asks you to, it is not a ward.

They are not medicine and they are not a promise. They are a way of keeping a door shut, and of remembering that you are the one who holds the key.

— M.A.

I

The salt line

A threshold remembers who crossed it. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • A handful of plain salt
  • A broom
  • A quiet hour, before dark

How

  1. Sweep the threshold first, from the inside out, so nothing old is left lying there.
  2. Pour a thin line of salt across the inside of the door, from one side of the frame to the other. Do not leave a gap.
  3. Say it plainly, once: nothing passes here that I did not ask in.

How to close

In the morning, sweep the salt outward over the step and let the weather take it.

Never use that salt again. Never cook with it.

II

The candle and the closed circle

A circle with a gap in it is only a door. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • One plain white candle
  • A fireproof dish to stand it in
  • Salt
  • A window you can open

How

  1. Stand the candle in the dish on a clear table, far from cloth and paper. Never on the floor.
  2. Draw a ring of salt around the dish, and close it. Walk the ring once with your eyes before you light the wick.
  3. Sit with the flame and keep your own counsel. Nothing is invited. You are only keeping the light company.
  4. Never leave a flame alone in a room, not even for a moment.

How to close

Snuff the flame, do not blow it out. Say: this is finished.

Open the window for a breath of air. Sweep the salt into the dish and carry it outside, away from the door.

III

Iron by the door

Iron has stood guard at doors longer than anyone remembers. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • An old iron key, or an iron nail
  • A length of red thread

How

  1. Thread the key on the red thread and tie the ends with three knots.
  2. Hang it inside the front door, above the frame, where it can see whoever enters.
  3. Touch it when you come home late. That is all it asks.

How to close

Once a year, take it down. Wash it in cold, clean water, dry it well so it does not rust, and hang it again on new thread.

IV

Rosemary and bay

Green things kept at the edges of a house. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • A sprig of rosemary
  • Three dried bay leaves
  • A small cloth bag or a dish

How

  1. Lay the rosemary along the top of a door frame or a window ledge.
  2. Put the bay leaves in the cloth bag and keep it near where you sleep, or under the mat at the door.
  3. Breathe the rosemary in when you pass it. Let the house smell like someone lives there.

How to close

When the leaves are dry and brittle, return them to the earth: bury them, or give them to the garden.

Put fresh ones in their place. A ward that is forgotten is no ward.

V

Closing a session

Whatever you open, you close. Out loud. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • Every light in the room
  • A glass of water
  • Your own name

How

  1. When you are done with a reading, with a song played too loud too late, with a night spent too long in the dark, do not simply walk away.
  2. Say plainly that it is over: this is finished, and everything goes back where it came from.
  3. Close what you opened. The book, the lid, the box. Put the cards away face down. Cover the mirror if you uncovered it.

How to close

Turn on every light. Drink the water slowly. Wash your hands in cold water.

Say your own name once, as if you were calling yourself home.

VI

Grounding

Come back down into your own weight. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • Bare feet
  • Earth, stone or a plain floor
  • Something to eat, a little bread or salt

How

  1. Stand with your feet flat and apart. Let your weight sink into your heels.
  2. Breathe in slowly. Breathe out longer than you breathed in. Five times.
  3. Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch. Say them out loud.

How to close

Eat something small. Drink water. Step outside if you can, and look at something far away.

VII

Fresh air and water

Old air keeps old company. Let it out. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • Every window in the house
  • A bowl of cold water with a pinch of salt
  • A clean cloth

How

  1. Open every window, even in winter, even for one minute. Let the air run through from room to room.
  2. Dip the cloth and wipe the door handles, the window sills and the threshold, always moving toward the door.
  3. Leave the rooms you sleep in for last.

How to close

Pour the water out on the ground outside, away from the door. Rinse the bowl.

Close the windows from the top of the house to the bottom.

VIII

The covered mirror

A mirror at night is a window you forgot to close. Break the sealFold it shut

What you need

  • A dark cloth for each mirror that faces a bed

How

  1. At night, before you sleep, cover any mirror that can see you sleeping.
  2. Do not look into it in the dark to see what looks back.

How to close

Uncover it in the morning, when there is daylight in the room. Fold the cloth and keep it close.

If you are frightened, or unwell, a ward is not enough. Turn on the lights and talk to someone living: a friend, a doctor, someone you trust.
